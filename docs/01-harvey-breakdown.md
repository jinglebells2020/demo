## 1. What the video is

A **narrated product overview** ("platform tour"): one calm narrator, no on-camera people, no stock footage. Every second on screen is either (a) the real product UI, lightly animated, or (b) minimal brand graphics: a wordmark, a stat, a section title, an icon row, a diagram, a 3D logo object. The grammar is *tell the benefit → show the exact click that delivers it*.

It is built as a **modular sales asset**: five product pillars, each a self-contained 25–45 s chapter with its own title card, so any chapter can be cut out as a standalone clip.

## 4. Visual language

**Palette.** Warm off-white "paper" (≈ #F4F2ED) is the base; a muted sage-grey (≈ #D6DAD4) for section panels and device backdrops; near-black for text, the cube and two "dark" cards (Vault, 700+ deployments). Product UI is white on off-white with hairline borders. The only saturated colors are inside the product (playbook highlights, a red PDF chip, Microsoft brand icons). No gradients, no glow except the brief cube highlight.

**Typography.** Two voices: a classic **serif** for everything editorial (wordmark, stats "25", "96%", "7 Hours", section words "Think / Research / Draft", tagline) and the product's **sans** for anything that lives inside the UI. Big numerals are set very large with a tiny caption beside them, not under them. Sentence case throughout; no uppercase tracking.

**Layout.** Generous margins; one idea per frame; centered compositions for cards, left-aligned for UI. Device frames are simplified (thin bezels, rounded corners, soft contact shadow), never photographic. UI is shown at 100 % or zoomed in 1.3–1.6× to the region that matters. Section pills (icon + word) introduce chapters.

**Iconography.** Lucide-style black line icons in rounded squares (practice areas); circular badges for compliance; one 3D object (the cube) as the brand's physical avatar.

**UI treatment.** Real product, real data, cursor visible, states change on click. No fake dashboards, no blurred placeholders. Text is legible at 1080p.

## 5. Motion and editing grammar

- **Rhythm.** 32 scenes / 232 s ≈ 7 s per scene. Fast in the hook (1–5 s), slow inside demos (9–15 s), fast again in the close.
- **Transitions.** Mostly straight cuts and quick cross-dissolves; panels "slide off" to reveal the UI; two hard cuts to black mark the Vault and proof sections.
- **Micro-motion.** Staggered pop-ins (icons, cards, columns), numeric counters, typing, cursor paths, list scrolls, colored highlight paints, node/edge draw-ons, one continuous 3D rotation. Easing is soft ease-out, ~300–500 ms; nothing bounces.
- **Camera.** Gentle push-ins and pans over UI (1.0→1.3×), occasional 2.5D layering of windows.
- **Continuity.** VO leads; the visual answers within ~0.5 s. Every claim in the VO has a literal on-screen counterpart (a number, a click, a label).

## 6. Audio

- **Narrator.** One calm, confident, mid-register voice; neutral corporate register; no jokes. ≈ 560 words in 3:44 of speech ≈ **150 wpm**; short declaratives; pauses at chapter boundaries (≈ 0.8–1.2 s).
- **Music.** Soft ambient/minimal bed under everything, swells for the final card, fades with the wordmark.
- **SFX.** None audible in the captions; if present they are subtle UI clicks.

## 7. Copywriting patterns (reuse these)

1. **Proof first, feature second**: three numbers in the first sentence (74,000 / 700 / 58).
2. **Benefit → mechanism**: "save up to 25 hours" → "access anywhere" → "enterprise-grade".
3. **Named worked example**: "For example, as a litigator, you can upload a complaint and…".
4. **Quantified magic**: "100 model calls", "100,000 files", "96 % vs 89 %".
5. **Verb triads**: think / research / draft; summarize / synthesize / draft; jurisdiction / practice area / objection format.
6. **Guardrails as features**: "encrypted, controlled, never used to train models".
7. **Close on scale + identity**: "700 deployments" → "trusted partner modernizing the legal industry" → tagline.
